It’s wonderful that you enjoy watching your pet fish—it sounds like a peaceful and rewarding part of your day! Fish behavior can be fascinating, and their habits often reflect their environment and well-being. Here’s a breakdown of what might be going on and how to enhance the experience for both you and your finned friend:

### Why Fish Swim in Circles:
1. **Natural Behavior**: Some fish, like goldfish or bettas, naturally swim in patterns. If the tank is circular or has a current from a filter, they might follow the flow.
2. **Curiosity or Exploration**: Fish often investigate their surroundings, especially if there are plants, decorations, or changes in the tank.
3. **Stress or Boredom**: Persistent, repetitive swimming (like "glass surfing") can sometimes indicate stress, poor water quality, or lack of stimulation. Check for signs like rapid gill movement, loss of appetite, or clamped fins.
4. **Health Issues**: If the fish is tilting or swimming erratically, it could signal a swim bladder problem or infection. A vet or aquarium expert can help if needed.

### Tips for a Happy, Engaging Tank:
- **Enrichment**: Add plants (live or artificial), caves, or toys to create a stimulating environment. Some fish enjoy nibbling on algae wafers or watching their reflection (though not for long!).
- **Water Quality**: Test pH, ammonia, and temperature regularly. Clean water is essential for health and happiness.
- **Lighting & Routine**: Fish thrive on consistency. A timer for lights and feeding can help them feel secure.
- **Interactive Moments**: Talk to your fish—they might recognize your voice! Some even learn to swim to the surface when you approach.

### Bonding with Your Fish:
Your routine of watching them before dinner is a lovely ritual. Studies show fish can recognize their owners and even respond to names (yes, really!). Taking time to observe them can be meditative and a great way to unwind.

If you ever notice changes in behavior or health, don’t hesitate to reach out to a vet or aquarium community for advice. For now, enjoy the little moments—your fish’s circles might just be their way of saying, “I’m happy here with you!” 🐠✨
