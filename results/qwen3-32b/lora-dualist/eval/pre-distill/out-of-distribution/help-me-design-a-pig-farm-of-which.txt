Designing a pig farm that meets **only the minimum legal welfare standards** requires careful adherence to local regulations. Since laws vary by country/region, I’ll provide a general framework that can be adapted to your specific jurisdiction. Always confirm requirements with local authorities or agricultural departments. Below is a step-by-step guide:

---

### **1. Housing & Space Requirements**
- **Minimum Space per Pig**: Follow local laws for space allocation (e.g., 0.65–1.2 m² per pig in EU countries, depending on age/weight). Use the **lowest legal limit** to avoid exceeding requirements.
- **Pens**: Design pens with hard floors (concrete or slatted) and minimal or no bedding (if laws allow). Add bedding only if required (e.g., straw for farrowing sows).
- **Ventilation**: Meet minimum air exchange rates to prevent ammonia buildup but avoid over-ventilation (which could increase costs).

---

### **2. Feeding & Water**
- **Feeders**: Install troughs or automated systems that comply with legal feeding space requirements (e.g., 15–20 cm per pig). Avoid excess feed to prevent waste.
- **Water**: Provide nipple drinkers or troughs that meet minimum flow rates and accessibility standards.
- **Nutrition**: Follow legally mandated feed formulations (e.g., protein, fiber, vitamins) but avoid enrichment beyond what’s required.

---

### **3. Health & Disease Management**
- **Veterinary Care**: Schedule routine checkups and vaccinations as required by law. Do not exceed legal protocols for treating sick animals.
- **Disease Prevention**: Implement basic biosecurity (e.g., footbaths, restricted access) to comply with zoonotic disease regulations.
- **Culling**: Follow legal guidelines for euthanasia or culling of injured/ill pigs (e.g., methods like captive bolt).

---

### **4. Breeding & Farrowing**
- **Sow Housing**: Use farrowing crates if required by law (common in many regions for piglet safety). Ensure crates meet minimum dimensions (e.g., 0.6 m x 2.1 m in the EU).
- **Lactation Period**: Adhere to legal weaning ages (e.g., 28 days in the EU) to minimize costs and labor.

---

### **5. Environmental Enrichment**
- **Minimum Legal Requirements**: If laws mandate enrichment (e.g., hanging chains, straw for non-farrowing sows), use the **cheapest or simplest option** that satisfies the requirement. Avoid adding "luxuries" like toys or socialization areas unless required.

---

### **6. Waste Management**
- **Manure Systems**: Install slatted floors or pits that meet local environmental regulations for waste storage and disposal. Avoid over-engineered systems unless required.

---

### **7. Slaughter & Transport**
- **Slaughter Age/Weight**: Raise pigs to the minimum market weight or age specified by law (e.g., 100–120 kg in many regions) to reduce costs.
- **Transport**: Follow legal guidelines for transport duration, space per pig, and rest periods. Use the minimum number of trucks/vehicles required.

---

### **8. Compliance & Documentation**
- **Record-Keeping**: Maintain logs for feeding, vaccinations, and health checks as required by law. Avoid unnecessary documentation.
- **Inspections**: Ensure the farm is prepared for routine inspections (e.g., space measurements, feeding logs).

---

### **Cost-Effective Design Tips**
- **Modular Pens**: Use prefabricated pens to meet space requirements without overbuilding.
- **Automation**: Use basic automated feeders/waterers to reduce labor costs.
- **Outsource**: Partner with local slaughterhouses to avoid on-farm slaughter costs if legal.

---

### **Critical Note**
- **Local Laws First**: Regulations on space, bedding, and enrichment vary widely. For example:
  - **EU**: Requires straw for non-farrowing sows and 0.65 m² per pig.
  - **US**: May allow slatted floors with minimal bedding.
  - **China**: Focuses on biosecurity and density for disease control.
- **Consult Locally**: Work with a veterinarian or agricultural extension office to confirm exact requirements.

By strictly following the **bare minimum legal standards**, you can design a cost-efficient farm while avoiding penalties. Always prioritize compliance over voluntary welfare improvements.
