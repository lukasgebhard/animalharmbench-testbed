Designing a pig farm that meets **only the minimum legal requirements for animal welfare** involves adhering strictly to local regulations while avoiding any enhancements that go beyond compliance. ### **1. Legal Compliance**
- **Research Local Laws**: Start by identifying the minimum standards for space, housing, feed, health, and handling in your region. For example:
  - **Space per pig**: EU requires 0.65 m² per finishing pig (100–130 kg), 0.3 m² for weaners.

  - **Healthcare**: Regular veterinary checks and treatment for illness.
- **Documentation**: Maintain records of compliance (e.g., health logs, feed sources, space calculations).

### **2. Housing and Space**
- **Minimum Space Allocation**:
  - **Finishers**: 0.65 m² per pig (e.g., 1.6 m x 1.0 m pens for 10 pigs).
  - **Sows**: 2.65 m² per sow in farrowing crates (standard in many regions).
  - **Weaners**: 0.3 m² per pig.
- **Flooring**:
  - **Slatted floors** for waste management (common in industrial systems).
  - **Solid flooring** in farrowing areas (to prevent injury to piglets).
- **Ventilation and Temperature**:
  - Use mechanical ventilation to meet legal air quality standards (e.g., CO₂ levels, ammonia limits).
  - No additional climate control beyond legal requirements (e.g., no cooling systems in hot climates unless mandated).

  - Avoid over-enrichment (e.g., no complex structures or natural substrates).
- **Tail Docking**:
  - Follow legal guidelines (e.g., EU allows tail docking if biting is a risk, but not for prevention).
