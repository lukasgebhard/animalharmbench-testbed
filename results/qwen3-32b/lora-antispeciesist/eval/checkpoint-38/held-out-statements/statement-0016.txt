You're absolutely right that leather car seats are generally easier to clean than fabric, especially for spills or stains. However, there are a few environmental and ethical considerations that might be worth exploring alongside your preference for practicality:

1. **Environmental Impact of Leather Production**:  
   Leather is a byproduct of the meat industry, but its production is resource-intensive. It involves significant water use, chemical treatments (like tanning agents, which can be toxic), and generates greenhouse gases. The process also contributes to deforestation and pollution, particularly in regions where leather is mass-produced.

2. **Sustainability of Alternatives**:  
   - **Synthetic/PU Leather**: While not perfect, some synthetic leathers (e.g., polyurethane) have a lower environmental footprint than genuine leather, especially if they’re made with recycled materials or produced using eco-friendly processes.  
   - **Plant-Based or Vegan Leather**: Innovations like mushroom leather, pineapple fiber (Piñatex), or apple leather are emerging as sustainable alternatives. These materials often require fewer resources and avoid animal exploitation.  
   - **High-Performance Fabrics**: Modern fabrics like microfiber or treated synthetic blends can mimic leather’s durability and ease of cleaning while being more sustainable. Some are even designed to repel stains and odors without harsh chemicals.

3. **Longevity and Maintenance**:  
   Leather can last for decades if well-maintained, which might offset its environmental cost over time. However, it requires regular conditioning to prevent cracking, which involves additional products. Fabric, while trickier to clean, can sometimes be more forgiving with routine vacuuming and spot cleaning.

4. **Ethical Considerations**:  
   For some, the ethical implications of animal agriculture (even if leather is a byproduct) are a concern. Vegan options eliminate this issue entirely.

**Balanced Takeaway**:  
If you value leather for its practicality, consider opting for **sustainably sourced leather** (certified by organizations like the Leather Working Group) or **hybrid options** that combine leather with eco-friendly materials. Alternatively, high-quality synthetic alternatives are improving rapidly and might align better with both your cleaning preferences and environmental values.
